# Article / longread rubric (70 points + 30 core)

Applies to: blog posts, guides, playbooks, newsletter issues, anything over ~600 words meant
to be read start to finish.

| # | Category | Pts | 10/10 looks like | 0 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **Title + first 100 words** | 12 | The title names the method or the result, not the topic. The first paragraph gives the reader a reason to keep going: a number, a situation they recognise, a claim they want to see proven. | "Thoughts on outbound"; an intro that explains what the article will explain. |
| A2 | **One argument, carried** | 14 | One thesis, stated early, and every section either proves it or applies it. A reader can say in one sentence what the article claims. | A list of loosely related tips; sections that could be reordered without loss. |
| A3 | **Evidence over assertion** | 14 | Numbers with denominators, named situations (neutral: "a 40-person BIM firm"), before/after, screenshots or tables where a claim needs them. Every "usually" has a "how often". | "Most companies", "in my experience" with nothing attached; borrowed frameworks with someone else's numbers. |
| A4 | **Structure serves scanning** | 10 | Headings say what the section concludes, not what it is about. A reader who only reads headings gets the argument. Tables and lists where the content is parallel; prose where it is not. | "Introduction / Body / Conclusion"; walls of prose; lists of one item. |
| A5 | **Transfer: reader can act** | 12 | The reader leaves with a step they can take Monday, with the inputs named. If a tool or skill is mentioned, the exact way to get it is there. | Inspiration without instruction; "consider reviewing your process". |
| A6 | **Length earns itself** | 8 | Nothing could be cut without losing an argument or an example. Paragraphs of different lengths. | Repetition of the intro in the outro; padding; the same idea in three phrasings. |

**Type hard fails:** a borrowed numbered framework presented as the author's; a conclusion
that introduces a new claim; a title that promises a number the body never gives.

**Judge input:** the article text (markdown or plain), its intended reader in one line, this rubric, core.md.
