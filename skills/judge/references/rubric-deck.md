# Deck / presentation rubric (70 points + 30 core)

Applies to: call decks, workshop decks, audit presentations, course slides, any HTML or PDF
deck. Judge reads the slides in order, once, at the speed of a person in a meeting.

| # | Category | Pts | 10/10 looks like | 0 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **One idea per slide** | 12 | Each slide's title is the takeaway ("Replies dropped because the list was wrong"), and the slide proves only that. Nothing needs the speaker to be understood. | Topic titles ("Results"); slides with two arguments; bullets that are notes for the speaker. |
| D2 | **Narrative arc** | 12 | Situation → what is broken → what we did / propose → what it changes → one decision asked of the audience. The order could not be shuffled. | A tour of everything the author knows; the ask appears nowhere or on slide 2. |
| D3 | **Numbers are the slide** | 14 | The chart or the number IS the slide, with the denominator and the period. Before/after where a change is claimed. The "positive" colour only on genuinely positive outcomes. | Decorative charts; percentages without bases; a table pasted whole. |
| D4 | **Density and legibility** | 10 | Under ~40 words per slide, one visual, 16:9 (or the requested format), readable from a laptop across a table. Cyrillic renders in the intended font. | Paragraphs on slides; 8-point tables; two fonts fighting. |
| D5 | **Brand fidelity** | 10 | A deck for a client → the client's design system, every token. Your own deck → your design system. Never both. | Your accent colour on a client deck; off-palette backgrounds; a wrong or distorted logo. |
| D6 | **The ask** | 12 | The last slide asks for ONE decision, with what happens next and by when. The audience knows what to say yes to. | "Thank you"; "Questions?"; three asks. |

**Type hard fails:** a client's design system mixed with your own tokens; a
number without a period or a base on a results slide; final slide with no decision.

**Judge input:** the deck (HTML/PDF/screenshots in order), audience in one line ("CEO of a
40-person BIM firm, first call"), which design system applies, this rubric, core.md.
